## Exact atlas prompt

Asset type: transparent pixel-art game animation sprite sheet for the approved small Alexandrine parakeet. Reference 1 is the APPROVED character design, scale/pose on the monitor. Reference 2 is the APPROVED preening pose and enlarged pixel appearance. Render ONLY THE PARROT, no monitor or background. Output ONE SQUARE sprite sheet with exactly 4 equal columns and 4 equal rows = 16 frames in strict row-major playback order on a truly transparent background, no grid lines, captions, numbers, labels or cast shadow. Each equal square cell must contain exactly one COMPLETE, isolated parrot, no clipping. Maintain EXACT same character scale, pixel size, silhouette style, palette, body/wing/maroon patch markings, tail length and two-foot placement across ALL frames. At a 1024-square sheet, each cell is 256x256; use crisp flat pixel clusters on an effective 64x64 grid enlarged by 4, no antialiasing. Within EVERY cell, keep two gripping feet located at exactly local x150 and x172, both soles at local y200; tip of resting tail around x28,y190; keep every pixel inside x20..224,y28..204. Two feet, body center and resting tail are registered to the same positions across every cell. The bird is a compact, modest-sized Alexandrine facing RIGHT, moss/olive green plumage, muted red short hooked beak, tiny dark eye, faint dark/rose collar, maroon shoulder patch, dark green folded wing, longish tail extending down-left, small ochre gripping feet. No branch or perch: feet curve over an imaginary horizontal monitor edge at local y200. Most frames use a forward-looking relaxed compact resting pose from reference 1. ROW 1, four frames: neutral rest; minimal breathing chest expansion by just one effective source pixel; soft blink with same breathing chest; neutral rest matching frame 1 exactly. ROW 2, frames 5-8: start preening slowly, head turns back LEFT toward visible maroon shoulder, neck curls down progressively, beak reaches shoulder wing feathers. Feet, torso, tail DO NOT translate or change size. ROW 3, frames 9-12: head tucked back touching shoulder as reference 2, eyes relaxed/closed; four tiny grooming beak/neck motions alternating touch and lift by one effective pixel, wing lifts at most one effective pixel. ROW 4, frames 13-16: head gently uncurls and turns toward the RIGHT again, returning progressively to original resting pose; final frame is identical to first resting frame. Do not make separate birds or change anatomy between frames. No alpha/dissolve ghosting, double faces, open wings, floating feet, bobbing whole body, stretching neck, feathers drawn individually, cartoon shine, light glows, sticker borders, smooth vector contours, gradients or extra detail. The entire background must be TRANSPARENT, not a simulated checkerboard. This is a usable aligned animation atlas, not an illustrated contact sheet.

## Rendering

- The actual atlas is 1254 x 1254. Cell edges are rounded from its actual dimensions, rather than assuming the requested 1024 pixels.
- `scripts/inspect-parrot-atlas.py` reads its alpha bounds and ochre foot positions into `atlas-registration.json`; it does not modify the PNG.
- A review-only compositor registers the feet to room coordinates (630,317), draws onto a 96 x 96 native canvas, and disables smoothing. Hard alpha removes the faint generated fringe at display time. All frames use the same scale.
- The original 1672 x 942 world transform handles cropping, camera entry/return, resizing and phone panning.
- The resting/breathing/blink cycle lasts four seconds. Preening uses the authored poses with holds at eight ticks per second, lasts three seconds, and occurs after about fourteen idle seconds or on the review button.
- Lighting dims/tints the isolated sprite in the dark and follows the existing lamp activation clip. No room patch is inserted. No pose crossfade is used.
- Reduced motion holds the neutral pose. Manual pause and a frame scrubber are review tools. Animation pauses outside the room and when the document is hidden.

Generated frames can still contain small shape variations. The contact sheet, actual room video, close-up and frame controls are provided for the user's visual decision; passing functional tests is not a claim of perfect art consistency.
